Abeceda · the alphabet

Croatian is spelled the way it sounds: one letter, one sound, every letter pronounced. Learn these 30 letters and you can read anything aloud. Tap a letter to hear its example word.

Red letters are the ones English speakers need to learn. Lj, Nj and Dž count as single letters. Q, W, X and Y appear only in foreign words.

Five tips for sounding right

Vowels are short and pure. a as in father, e as in bed, i as in machine, o as in more, u as in food. They never glide the way English vowels do.

Roll the r. It can even act as a vowel: trg (square), prst (finger), the island of Krk.

Č vs Ć. Č is hard, tongue back (čaj). Ć is soft, tongue forward (kuća). Many Croatians blur them, so don't worry too much.

Stress usually falls early, often on the first syllable, and never on the last syllable of a longer word: HRvatska, dobar dan.

J is always a y sound. Jaje (egg) is "YAH-yeh".

Present tense: three patterns

Most verbs follow one of three endings. Pronouns are usually dropped, because the ending already tells you who: Govorim engleski means "I speak English".

To make a verb negative, put ne in front: Ne razumijem (I don't understand). Three verbs merge with it: nisam (I'm not), nemam (I don't have), neću (I won't / don't want).

Ti or Vi?

Use ti with friends, family, children and people your age in casual settings. Use Vi with strangers, older people, shop and restaurant staff. If you're unsure, use Vi. You'll hear Kako ste? (formal) and Kako si? (informal), and Izvolite from anyone serving you.

To ask a yes/no question, put the verb first and add li: Govorite li engleski? (Do you speak English?), Imate li sobu? (Do you have a room?). With "to be", use je li: Je li daleko? (Is it far?).
